--- PAGE 1 ---
EXT. RIDGELINE TRAIL - DAWN

A hiking trail cuts through dry scrubland above a small desert town. DETECTIVE ELEANOR VOSS, 44, climbs at a punishing pace. She wears a battered Dodgers cap. Her holster is visible under her jacket. She stops at a rock outcropping and looks down at the town.

Her phone buzzes. She reads the screen. Her face goes flat.

VOSS
(to herself)
Not again.

--- PAGE 2 ---
INT. CRIME SCENE - ABANDONED WAREHOUSE - MORNING

Yellow tape. Forensics in white suits. Voss ducks under the tape. Her partner, DETECTIVE RAY OKONKWO, 38, meets her halfway across the concrete floor. He hands her coffee in a paper cup.

OKONKWO
Third one this month. Same signature — left shoe removed, laces tied around the wrist.

VOSS
Victim?

OKONKWO
Male. Mid-thirties. Wallet's clean, no ID. But we found a key card in the left sock. Keystone Properties.

Voss crouches near the body. She studies the wrist.

VOSS
He wasn't running. Look at the posture. He sat down for this.

--- PAGE 3 ---
INT. RIDGELINE POLICE DEPARTMENT - CONFERENCE ROOM - DAY

Voss pins a photograph to the board. The victim: gaunt, dark-haired, a small tattoo of a compass rose on his right forearm.

CAPTAIN DIANA BELL, 55, stands at the window. Arms crossed.

BELL
The mayor called this morning. Third body means press. Press means pressure. You have forty-eight hours before I bring in the state task force.

VOSS
I don't need forty-eight hours. Give me the property records on Keystone.

BELL
Already pulled. Keystone Properties is a shell. Registered in Nevada, director listed as a Thomas Wren. No current address.

Okonkwo enters. He sets a printout on the table.

OKONKWO
The key card accesses a storage unit on Route 9. Unit forty-four.

--- PAGE 4 ---
INT. RIDGELINE POLICE DEPARTMENT - VOSS'S OFFICE - DAY

Voss spreads the files across her desk. Three victims, three sets of photographs. She aligns them chronologically.

Her desk phone rings. She picks up.

VOSS
Voss.

VOICE (V.O.)
Detective. You should look more carefully at the tattoo.

VOSS
Who is this?

The line goes dead. Voss looks at the photograph of the victim's right forearm. The compass rose. She picks up a magnifying glass.

Inside the compass — nearly invisible at normal resolution — a six-digit number. She writes it on a notepad: 441892.

--- PAGE 5 ---
EXT. ROUTE 9 STORAGE FACILITY - DAY

Voss and Okonkwo pull into the gravel lot. The facility is half-empty, chain-link fence, a guardhouse with a bored attendant.

They badge in and walk to unit forty-four.

OKONKWO
You sure about this? No warrant.

VOSS
Key card was on the body. Exigent circumstances.

She slides the key card. The lock clicks. They lift the rolling door.

Inside: filing boxes, stacked four high. And in the center of the floor, a laptop, open, screen dark.

Okonkwo pulls on gloves and touches the trackpad. The screen wakes. A single document is open. At the top: a list of names. Twelve names. The third name down is ELEANOR VOSS.

Voss stares at her own name on the screen.

Okonkwo pulls out his radio. His left hand reaches for it instinctively — the same hand that was bandaged when they arrived at the crime scene that morning.
